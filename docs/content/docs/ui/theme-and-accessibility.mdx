## Appearance and interface style

Two settings decide the look (D44). Both are in View ▸ Appearance, View ▸
Interface Style and the palette.
- **Appearance:** Auto (follows macOS), Light or Dark. Dark is the default.
- **Interface style:** Glass or Classic. Glass is the default.
  - Glass: the window background is a behind-window blur under a tint, so
    the desktop shows through softly. The selected tab and workspace row are
    Liquid Glass capsules.
  - Classic: an opaque window with no blur. The selected tab and row are
    plain raised pills.
- In both styles the sidebar sits flat on the window background, and pane
  cards are translucent fills with thin edges, never glass. Popovers, the
  palette, the hover card, the Agents popover, the find bar and notices use
  the system glass over the system's regular material, so a terminal behind
  them is only a soft blur (on a small panel Liquid Glass alone let its text
  read through). Tooltips are the system's.
- Pane cards have a 10 pt radius. The 8 pt gap between cards is the one fixed
  layout size.

One token table in `Style.swift` holds every color the views draw, per style
and appearance, with values measured from Rex and tuned by eye. In short:

| Token | Glass | Classic |
|---|---|---|
| Window background | behind-window blur (`hudWindow`) + a tint: `#1e1e1e` at 0.5 in dark, with black 0.6 blended `overlay` under it to darken the blur; white at 0.7 in light | opaque `#1e1e1e` dark, `#f6f6f6` light |
| Card fill | white 0.03 dark, 0.6 light, a step brighter than the window; the keyboard card only a little brighter (white 0.02 more in dark, 0.25 in light, so it ends at 0.7) | white 0.018 dark (`#222` on `#1e1e1e`), 0.6 light; a step apart on the keyboard card (black 0.1 more in dark, 0.8 in light) |
| Card edges | Rex's rim, `plusLighter`: a 0.7 pt inner rim (white 0.06 dark, 0.04 light) and over it along the top a 1 pt line (white 0.09 dark, 0.3 light) that fades out round the corners over 14 pt, with a glow under it (8 pt, about half); then a 1 pt outer stroke (black 0.5 dark, 0.1 light, the same on every card); in light a shadow (black 0.1, blur 5, 2 pt down) | light: a 0.7 pt outer stroke, 0.8 pt and stronger on the keyboard card; dark: only the keyboard card has one (0.8 pt, white 0.1); no shadow and no rim, as Rex's Classic cards |
| Selected tab and row | Liquid Glass capsule | a flat pill (white 0.045) in dark; white with a fine stroke and a shadow in light |
| Tab track | the primary color at 0.05 (0.1 with Increase Contrast) | the same in light; none in dark |
| Title row and sidebar text and glyphs | white 0.65 dark, black 0.65 light (0.85 with Increase Contrast); the selected tab's and row's title white 0.9 / black 0.9 (1 with Increase Contrast) | the same |
| Tab dividers | white 0.17 dark, black 0.14 light, as in Rex (0.3 and 0.28 with Increase Contrast) | the same |
| Card header buttons and the divider grip | white 0.42 dark, black 0.45 light (0.6 and 0.65 with Increase Contrast): at least 3:1 on the card and the window | the same |

The hover and pressed fills (rows, tabs, buttons) are the same in both styles:
the primary color at 0.05 and 0.08, which follows the appearance by itself, so
they are not tokens. The hover fill fades in and out over 0.14 s; a menu's
highlight (the palette, the workspace menu, Open Session) moves at once.

Terminals keep a transparent background, so the card shows through. Their
text uses libghostty's defaults: Alabaster in light, Afterglow in dark. They
follow the appearance. A change updates every terminal without opening its
stream again. Selected text keeps its own color over a blue like macOS's
(`#B3D7FF` in light, `#1B3A58` in dark), except reverse-video text; the
defaults drew it in the terminal's background color, close to their grey
selection. There are no other themes.

## Status

The status words and colors are the same everywhere (tabs, workspace rows,
the Agents view and popover, the hover card, notifications):

| Shown as | Word | Dark | Light | herdr status |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| three working dots | working | `#58a6ff` | `#0667d8` | `working` |
| an amber badge on the program icon, an amber dot on a workspace row | needs you | `#d29922` | `#9d5c00` | `blocked` |
| a green badge on the program icon, a green dot on a workspace row | finished | `#3fb950` | `#116329` | finished and not yet seen by this device |
| ↓behind on a workspace row, the error tone of a notice | error | `#f85149` | `#cf222e` | — |

- **Working dots.** Three dots, 3.5 pt, 5.5 pt apart, in a 14 pt box. Each
  dot's opacity goes 0.3 → 0.85 → 0.3 over 1.2 s, each dot 0.2 s after the
  one before. The wall clock drives them, so every tab is in step. Core
  Animation runs them at 15 frames a second or less, so the app does no work
  per frame (a SwiftUI timeline per dots laid the window out at every tick).
- **Badges.** A 7 pt dot on the top right of a tab's front program icon and
  of an Agents row's tile, cut out of the tile by a 1.5 pt ring.
- In the Agents view and popover and the hover card, status is a capsule:
  the word in the color, on a 0.15 fill of it. The Agents view also shows
  "Idle" in gray.
- A workspace row shows an idle agent as a 7 pt gray ring. Tabs show nothing
  for idle agents.

## Accessibility

- Status is never color alone. Every dot and badge carries its word, and
  VoiceOver reads, for example, "codex, needs you" on a tab. A workspace
  row adds the word to its value.
- A terminal card gives VoiceOver only its grid size, not its text.
- ⌥⌘ and an arrow moves the keyboard between cards. Keyboard focus in the
  chrome follows Full Keyboard Access. A terminal keeps Tab for itself.
- VoiceOver's actions on a card are its menu's items, with Move Pane to Tab's
  New Tab and New Workspace only. With any accessibility client running
  (BetterTouchTool, Raycast, a test), SwiftUI builds a card's actions with the
  card: with one per tab, a switch to a session of 300 tabs took three times
  as long. VO-Shift-M on a card opens its menu, with
  every tab; the palette lists them too.
- Every card action can be reached from the keyboard, through the menu bar and
  the palette (D48). Every swipe has a keyboard path, a menu path and a
  button. Hover controls are also in context menus and the menu bar.
- Each divider is an adjustable element: "Divider between ‹a› and ‹b›", with
  the ratio as a percent ([Dividers](/docs/layout#dividers)).
- The terminal font size is the app's text size: 13 pt by default, 8 to 32,
  with View ▸ Increase Font Size (⌘+ or ⌘=), Decrease Font Size (⌘-) and Reset
  Font Size (⌘0)
  ([Settings](/docs/ui/settings)).
- Reduce Motion stops the working dots (three still dots at 0.6), makes the
  tab selection and the strip jump, and turns off the icon bounce, the layout
  motion, the reorder slides, the spring-load pulse and the
  panels' scale-in.
- Reduce Transparency is handled by the system glass. Classic is an opaque
  window for users who want no blur at all.
- Increase Contrast makes the app's own dim colors stronger (the table above:
  the title row's text, tab dividers, the tab track, card header buttons and
  the grip). The system's colors follow it by themselves.
- The card header buttons and the divider grip have a contrast of at least
  3:1 against what is behind them, in both styles and both appearances
  (measured 3.3:1 to 4.1:1 on CI's desktop; in Glass it depends on the desktop).

## Keyboard shortcuts

The palette and the menu bar come from one command list, so each shortcut is
defined once. A command with two shortcuts shows the first in the menu; a key
monitor runs the first's command for the second (⇧⌘P, ⌃⇥, ⌃⇧⇥, ⌘=), always on
the key window's own command list. In a text field ⌃⇥ does nothing, so it never moves the keyboard to the next
terminal.

One pattern for every level: the key says the action, the modifier says the
level. `T` makes, `W` closes, `[` and `]` step, `1` to `9` jump. ⌘ is the pane
(and the tab for New Tab and the jumps), ⇧⌘ the tab, ⌃⌘ the workspace, ⌥⌘ the
session. Arrows always mean panes.

| | Pane | Tab | Workspace | Session |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| New | ⌘D right, ⇧⌘D down | ⌘T | ⌃⌘T | ⌥⌘T (Open Session) |
| Close | ⌘W (the tab too when it is the last pane) | ⇧⌘W | ⌃⌘W | ⌥⌘W (Detach Session) |
| Previous, next | ⌘[ ⌘] | ⇧⌘[ ⇧⌘], ⌃⇧⇥ ⌃⇥ | ⌃⌘[ ⌃⌘] | ⌥⌘[ ⌥⌘] |
| Jump to 1–9 | | ⌘1–9 | ⌃⌘1–9 | ⌥⌘1–9 |
| Swipe | | sideways on the strip | three fingers up or down | three fingers sideways, or sideways on the sidebar |

Pane steps follow the split tree, as in Ghostty: left before right, top before
bottom, round the ends. Tab and workspace steps go round the ends too,
workspaces in the sidebar's order; session steps stop at the first and last
page. A split gives the keyboard to the new pane.

**Keyboard Shortcuts panel.** ⌘/ (Help ▸ Keyboard Shortcuts, also in the
palette) opens a glass panel over the window, like the palette. It lists
every command with a shortcut, read from the command list so it never goes
stale, in two columns grouped as Panes, Tabs, Workspaces, Sessions, View and
Edit. Each modifier and key is its own keycap. A command's second shortcut
follows after "or". A set of commands that differ only by the key shows as
one row with one keycap for the keys: "Select Pane ⌥ ⌘ ←→↑↓", "Show Tab 1–9
⌘ 1–9". Esc, ⌘/ or a click outside closes it.

| Keys | Action | Menu |
|---|---|---|
| ⌘N | New Window, on the same machine | File |
| ⌘T | new tab, in the keyboard card's folder | File |
| ⌃⌘T | new workspace | File |
| ⌥⌘T | Open Session (the panel) | File |
| ⇧⌘G | Go to Directory (in a card that runs only its shell) | File |
| ⌘W | close the pane (asks first unless the pane runs only its shell) | File |
| ⇧⌘W | close the tab (asks first when a program runs in it) | File |
| ⌃⌘W | close the workspace (asks first when a program runs in it) | File |
| ⌥⌘W | Detach Session: the page leaves the window, the session keeps running | File |
| ⇧⌘I | Insert Path | Edit |
| ⌘F | find in the keyboard card | Edit |
| ⌘G, or Enter in the find bar | next match (⇧Enter: previous match) | Edit |
| ⌘K | the command palette; ⇧⌘P also opens it (Rex's key) | View |
| ⌘. | Show Agents: the Agents view on or off | View |
| ⌃⌘S | show or hide the sidebar | View |
| ⌃⌘O | Show Overview: the overview on or off | View |
| ⌘D, ⇧⌘D | split right, split down | View ▸ Pane |
| ⇧⌘↩ | zoom or restore the card | View ▸ Pane |
| ⌥⌘= | Balance Splits: even out the splits of the tab | View ▸ Pane |
| ⌥⌘ + arrow | Select Pane Left, Right, Up, Down: the keyboard goes to the next card that way (as in Ghostty and iTerm2) | View ▸ Pane |
| ⌘[ and ⌘] | Select Previous Pane, Select Next Pane | View ▸ Pane |
| ⌥⇧⌘ + arrow | Move Pane: swap the keyboard card with the card that way | View ▸ Pane |
| ⌘+ (and ⌘=), ⌘-, ⌘0 | Increase, Decrease, Reset Font Size: the text size | View |
| ⌃⌘F | full screen (the macOS key) | View |
| ⌘/ | Keyboard Shortcuts (the panel) | Help |
| ⌘1 … ⌘9 | switch tabs | Window |
| ⇧⌘[ and ⌃⇧⇥, ⇧⌘] and ⌃⇥ | previous and next tab | Window |
| ⇧⌘←, ⇧⌘→ | move the tab left, right (as iTerm2; in a text field they still select to the line's start or end) | Window |
| ⌃⌘[ and ⌃⌘] | previous and next workspace | Window |
| ⌃⌘1 … ⌃⌘9 | switch workspaces, in the sidebar's order | Window |
| ⌥⌘[ and ⌥⌘] | previous and next session | Window |
| ⌥⌘1 … ⌥⌘9 | switch sessions, in the window's order | Window |
| hold ⌘ for 0.7 s | keycap hints on the first nine tabs and workspace rows | |
| hold ⌃⌘ for 0.7 s | keycap hints on the first nine workspace rows | |

Close Window, Float Pane and Resize Pane (View ▸ Pane, the palette) have no
shortcut: the window's close button closes it, the card header's button
floats a pane, and the dividers and Balance Splits cover resizing. In the palette: ↩ runs, ⎋ goes back or closes, ⌫ on an empty
field goes back. Keyboard focus among the chrome's buttons follows the
system's Full Keyboard Access setting.

The menu bar redraws whenever a command's title, key, or enabled state
changes: SwiftUI rebuilds the menu bar only when the window's command list
changes, and a stale disabled Zoom Pane once ate ⇧⌘↩ after a split.

Rex shortcuts not taken, and why:
- ⌘K stays the palette (D48), so Change Session (⌘K in Rex) is not taken;
  sessions open with Open Session (⌥⌘T). ⇧⌘P also opens it (Rex's key).
- ⌘/ (sidebar) is Keyboard Shortcuts instead: ⌃⌘S is the macOS sidebar key.
- ⌘R (refresh) is not needed: the snapshot is live.
- Find Previous has no shortcut: ⇧⌘G is Go to Directory.
- ⇧⌘← and ⇧⌘↑ (Split Left, Split Up) would steal select-to-line-start from
  text fields.

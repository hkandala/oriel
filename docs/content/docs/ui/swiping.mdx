---
title: "Swiping"
description: "Session pages in the sidebar and tab pages in the strip"
---

- Two-finger swipe on a trackpad. Pages snap.
- **Sidebar.** A sideways swipe on the sidebar moves to the next or previous
  session page open in the window ([Sidebar](/docs/ui/window#sidebar)). The
  page dots show where you are, and a click on a dot switches. At the first
  or last page the swipe does nothing. In the Agents view the same swipe
  shows the next session's agents.
- **Strip.** A sideways swipe on the strip pages between the selected
  workspace's tabs (also ⇧⌘[ and ⇧⌘], ⌃⇥).
- **Three fingers.** A three-finger swipe sideways moves between sessions:
  it moves the sidebar's pages as a swipe over them does, or with the sidebar
  hidden, or in the overview, steps one session, as ⌥⌘[ ] do. Fingers to the
  left show the next session, as the system's own swipes. Up or down it moves
  what the sidebar shows: the workspace above or below, as ⌃⌘[ ] do, or in
  the Agents view the agent row above or below the lit one, going to its pane
  as a click on the row does (a folded group's rows and floated panes are
  skipped; the steps follow the list as it reorders, as clicks do). With the
  sidebar hidden, or in the overview, up and down step the workspace.
  Fingers up go to the row above, down to the one below, whatever the scroll
  direction. One step a gesture, as the fingers move 20 pt (on the
  trackpad) mostly one way, when the system's three-finger swipes are off
  (Trackpad settings: Swipe between full-screen apps, Mission Control and App
  Exposé on four fingers or off; no three-finger drag). macOS sends no scroll
  for three fingers: the app reads the trackpad's touches (`NSTouch`), which
  it gets once a view in the window takes indirect touches (the window's
  frame view, not SwiftUI's content view, which handles touches of its own).
  AppKit's `swipe(with:)` would need Swipe between pages on three fingers, and
  no gesture recognizer sees the trackpad. With the system's three-finger
  gestures on, the system takes the swipe; touches it cancels before the
  app's 60 ms and 20 pt step nothing (not tried on a trackpad with them on).
  Under a panel, in a floating pane's window or a sheet a three-finger swipe
  steps nothing. The UI says nothing about the setting. With ⌥ held a swipe scrolls as
  without it.
- **One target.** The app's scroll monitor locks a gesture to one axis (D35).
  It gives a sideways gesture over the sidebar (its pages, the dots and the
  filter) to the sidebar's pages, and elsewhere to the innermost scroll view
  under the pointer that can scroll sideways (its content is wider than its
  frame): over the strip, the strip. So swiping the sidebar never pages the
  strip, and the reverse. Under a panel (the palette, Open Session) the
  sidebar's pages do not move.
- A page follows the fingers one to one: the sidebar's session pages, the
  strip's tab pages and the overview's session pages alike. As the fingers
  lift it slides on to the page it lands on, starting at the fingers' speed:
  the next page that way, or the nearest page when they move slowly (under
  100 pt a second) or stop before they lift. The sidebar's pages slide with a
  spring of response 0.2 s and no bounce (0.14 s felt too quick). The strip and the overview
  start at the fingers' speed, speed up only as much as at most 0.28 s needs,
  and ease out; when the fingers' speed covers the rest in that time they only
  slow down. A spring from a slow lift jumped most of a page in two frames and
  looked cut. At the
  first and last page the pages stop. The app's scroll monitor gives the pages the gesture's moves and its lift and drops
  the momentum. The session pages are the app's own, not a scroll view's; the
  strip and the overview stay scroll views that the app scrolls. A scroll
  view's own paging snap is too slow (about 0.4 s) and sends a quick flick
  back to its page. At
  rest only the sidebar's page in view is laid out; the others come in while
  the pages move. A swipe during a slide takes the pages from where they are.
  The window shows the session, or selects the tab, once the slide looks
  done (the strip and the overview: once within a point of the page); shown at
  the lift, its first draw would hold the slide still. A
  wheel's sideways burst over the sidebar, the strip or the overview turns one
  page: the strip and the overview have no paging of their own (SwiftUI's
  snapped a swipe's first move to the next page). Under Reduce Motion they
  switch without motion. The keys, a click on a tab and the palette show the
  tab's page at once, with no slide.
- First visit to a session page: the app shows herdr's focused workspace and
  its active tab. Later visits: the workspace and tab the user last picked
  there, in that window.
- Every swipe also has a keyboard path, a menu path and a button. The keys are
  in [Keyboard shortcuts](/docs/ui/theme-and-accessibility#keyboard-shortcuts).
